\section{Business Model}\label{sec:business-model}

A business model describes how a company creates, delivers, and captures value.
We use the Business Model Canvas \cite{osterwalder2010}, which breaks the model into
nine connected blocks. This section first shows the whole canvas on one page, then
explains the choices that matter most: who Uvie serves, what value it offers, how it
earns money, and what could make the model fail.

\subsection{Customers and Value Proposition}

\subsubsection{Customer Segments}
The core segment is the same group sized in Section~\ref{sec:market-size}: urban
adults aged 18 to 35 who already use sunscreen and own a smartphone, about 4.5
million people in our estimate. We start with students and young workers because
they are online daily, price-sensitive, and spend long hours outdoors commuting.
Runners, cyclists, and other outdoor sports users form the first group of early
adopters, since they sweat sunscreen off quickly and gain the most from a clear
signal. Evidence from Australia shows that young people playing outdoor sport
respond well to UV-detection stickers \cite{horsham2020}. Parents of teenagers are a
possible later segment, but we do not include them in the forecast.

\subsubsection{Value Proposition}
A value proposition has to relieve a real pain, not just list features. Table~\ref{tab:vp}
connects the pains found in Section~\ref{sec:market-opportunities} to what Uvie
offers.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering\small
\caption{Customer pains and how Uvie responds}\label{tab:vp}
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{4.4cm}L{5.4cm}L{4.4cm}@{}}
  \toprule
  \textbf{Customer pain} & \textbf{Uvie's response} & \textbf{Benefit to the user}\\
  \midrule
  UV cannot be seen or felt, so users misjudge protection & Patch darkens when UV reaches the skin & Visible proof that sunscreen has worn off\\
  \addlinespace
  Users forget to reapply & App scan gives an explicit ``Reapply sunscreen'' alert & A prompt at the right moment\\
  \addlinespace
  Existing sticker packs are expensive in Vietnam & Locally priced package at VND 89,000 & Affordable for students and regular use\\
  \addlinespace

  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Uvie's main benefits are \emph{risk reduction} (fewer unnoticed burns),
\emph{convenience} (an answer in a few seconds), and \emph{price}. We do not claim a
medical benefit. The product is a reminder, and Section~\ref{sec:prototype} explains
why it should not be presented as a measurement of UV dose.

\subsection{Delivering and Capturing Value}

\subsubsection{Channels and Customer Relationships}
Customers first meet Uvie online. Short videos and creator reviews on TikTok lead
to a purchase on TikTok Shop or Shopee, where most of Vietnam's online retail
already takes place \cite{krasia2026}. The web app is both a product feature and a
sales channel: anyone can open it, try the demo mode, and understand the idea before
buying. In Year 2 we add pharmacies, and in Year 3 nationwide beauty chains, so
that customers who prefer to buy in person can also find the product. Details of
the sales plan are in Section~\ref{sec:marketing}.

Relationships are mostly self-service and community-based. The app guides use,
social media supplies tips and daily UV advice, and referral rewards turn happy
users into the cheapest acquisition channel, which is why Section~\ref{sec:financial}
assumes a falling acquisition cost.

\subsubsection{Revenue Model and Unit Economics}
Uvie sells patch packages. The app stays free so that nothing stops a customer from
trying it, which supports repeat purchases of patches. Table~\ref{tab:unit} shows the
unit economics used throughout this report.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering\small
\caption{Unit economics per package (base case, from Section~\ref{sec:financial})}\label{tab:unit}
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{7.2cm}r@{}}
  \toprule
  \textbf{Item} & \textbf{VND}\\
  \midrule
  Retail price & 89,000\\
  Direct cost (materials, processing, packaging, inspection) & 34,000\\
  Gross profit per package & 55,000\\
  Gross margin & 61.8\%\\
  Purchases per customer per year (assumption) & 3\\
  Customer lifetime (assumption) & 2 years\\
  Customer lifetime value (55,000 $\times$ 3 $\times$ 2) & 330,000\\
  Customer acquisition cost, Year 1 $\to$ Year 3 & 30,000 $\to$ 16,667\\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Two things stand out. The price is low in absolute terms but leaves a margin high
enough to pay for marketing, and the model depends heavily on repeat purchase:
a customer who buys once returns only VND 55,000 of gross profit against an
acquisition cost of VND 30,000 in Year 1. Repeat purchase is therefore the number to
watch.

Our small survey of 40 people gives a first check on the price (Appendix~\ref{app:survey}).
About 45.0\% said they would pay VND 89,000 or more per package, 47.5\% were likely to buy at
that price, and another 32.5\% would pay only between VND 50,000 and 89,000. The price
therefore suits about half of this sample. Because the sample is small and stated intention
tends to exceed real purchases, we treat the price as a hypothesis to test, and we would try a
lower entry price or a smaller pack if early sales are weak.

\paragraph{Other ways to earn.} Several extensions are possible, such as paid
premium features in the app, bulk supply to sports events, schools, and gyms, or
co-branded patches with sunscreen makers. We list them as options for later and
\emph{exclude them from the forecast}, in line with the conservative approach of
Section~\ref{sec:financial}.

\subsubsection{Cost Structure}
Uvie is a cost-conscious model. Variable cost is mainly materials and processing at
VND 34,000 per package, which gives a Year 1 cost of goods sold of VND 612 million.
Fixed costs are mostly people and marketing. In the base case operating expenses
before depreciation are VND 1,320 million in Year 1 and rise to VND 2,860 million in
Year 3, while revenue grows from VND 1,602 million to VND 8,900 million. The initial
funding requirement is VND 900 million. Full tables are in Section~\ref{sec:financial}.

\subsection{Key Resources, Activities, and Partners}
The resources that matter most are intangible: the patch formulation, the scan app,
and the data and know-how gathered from testing. Production and testing equipment is
the main physical asset. The key activities that Uvie keeps in-house are making and
checking patches, because quality drives trust, and developing the app. Activities
such as logistics, payment handling, and web hosting are left to partners.

\subsection{Risks to the Business Model}
A business model is a set of assumptions. Table~\ref{tab:bm-risk} names the ones
that, if wrong, would hurt Uvie most, together with how we plan to find out and what
to do about it.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering\small
\caption{Key assumptions and risks of the business model}\label{tab:bm-risk}
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{3.5cm}L{5.4cm}L{5.3cm}@{}}
  \toprule
  \textbf{Risk} & \textbf{Why it matters} & \textbf{Response}\\
  \midrule
  The patch does not track sunscreen wear-off reliably & Laboratory work shows photochromic devices can react quickly even under SPF 50+ at high UV \cite{aguilera2024}; unreliable signals would destroy trust & Test our patches under Vietnamese UV levels and with several SPF values; present Uvie as a reminder, not a dosimeter\\
  \addlinespace
  Low repeat purchase & LTV depends on three packages per year & Track cohorts early; add bundles and refill reminders\\
  \addlinespace
  Regulatory classification is unclear & A skin-contact product may need notification or testing; cosmetics, for example, must be notified to the health authority before sale \cite{circular06,mondaq2025} & Confirm the category with a legal adviser before launch; budget is in the legal and testing line\\
  \addlinespace
  Platform dependence & Most online sales run through two marketplaces \cite{krasia2026} & Build an owned audience through the app and social pages\\
  \addlinespace
  Intellectual property & The commercial sticker we benchmark is linked to a published patent application on its photochromic molecule \cite{horsham2020}, and Uvie's own mechanism is not protected & Check freedom to operate before launch; consider protecting our formulation; build brand trust\\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}
