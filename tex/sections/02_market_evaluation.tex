\subsection{Industry Analysis}

\subsubsection{Global and Vietnam Sun Care Market Size}
Vietnam's sun care market is growing quickly because of hot weather and stronger
daily skincare habits among young buyers. According to \citet{marketsdata2023},
Vietnam's sun care sector reached USD 124.12 million in 2022 and is forecast to
climb to USD 244.79 million by 2030, reflecting a high compound annual growth rate
(CAGR) of 8.86\% from 2023 to 2030 (Figure~\ref{fig:vn-market}).

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=4.3in]{figures/fig2-1_vietnam_sun_care_market.png}
  \caption{Vietnam Sun Care Market size and 8.86\% CAGR forecast (Source: \cite{marketsdata2023}).}
  \label{fig:vn-market}
\end{figure}

On a global scale, the sun care industry shows steady long-term expansion as
consumers become more aware of UV damage and premature skin aging. According to
\citet{imarc2026}, the global sun care products market rose from USD 11.49 billion
in 2020 to USD 14.81 billion in 2025, and it is projected to reach USD 19.09
billion in 2030 and USD 23.72 billion by 2034 at a 5.21\% CAGR
(Figure~\ref{fig:global-market}). Asia-Pacific holds the largest regional share
worldwide at 33.0\%.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=4.0in]{figures/fig2-2_global_sun_care_market.png}
  \caption{Global Sun Care Products Market growth trend from 2020 to 2034 (Source: \cite{imarc2026}).}
  \label{fig:global-market}
\end{figure}

Comparing both forecasts shows that Vietnam grows significantly faster than the
global average (8.86\% vs.\ 5.21\%), confirming strong regional demand inside
Asia-Pacific's 33.0\% share. Yet almost all current spending goes toward
sunscreen creams and sprays. Consumers still have few practical tools to verify
when their protection fades during outdoor study, travel, or sports.

\subsubsection{Key Market Demand Drivers}
Vietnam's tropical climate creates year-round demand for dependable UV protection.
Because the country sits close to the equator, major cities such as Ho Chi Minh
City, Da Nang, and Hanoi regularly record Very High to Extreme UV Index levels of
10 to 12+ \citep{who2022}. Unlike heat or bright sunlight, ultraviolet rays are
invisible, so people rarely realize skin damage is happening until redness or
tanning appears.

This invisibility causes a clear everyday skincare pain point. Skin experts at the
American Academy of Dermatology advise reapplying sunscreen every two hours, or
right after swimming and heavy sweating \citep{aad2023}. In practice, busy
university students and commuters easily forget their last application or assume a
morning layer lasts all afternoon. A wearable skin sticker that visibly shifts
color turns an invisible UV risk into a clear reminder.

Modern lifestyles and widespread smartphone access also support Uvie's adoption.
Sunscreen in Vietnam has grown into an everyday skincare step alongside booming
outdoor sports like running, cycling, and pickleball \citep{statista2024}. Since
young urban adults carry smartphones daily, Uvie works directly through a normal
camera and free web app (\href{https://sun-check.onrender.com}{sun-check.onrender.com})
without requiring extra electronic devices.

\subsubsection{Industry Barriers and Risks}
Developing a wearable UV sticker in Vietnam still involves several practical
technical challenges. In hot and humid outdoor conditions, the patch must stay
comfortably attached through sweat without peeling off early or irritating
sensitive skin. At the same time, the photochromic gel layer requires careful
testing so its color change matches real sunscreen wear-off accurately, while
camera scans remain consistent across different phones and sunlight angles.

From a commercial perspective, Uvie faces seasonal weather swings and customer
education barriers. Outdoor sun care spending tends to peak during bright sunny
months and slow down slightly during extended rainy seasons. Furthermore, UV
patches remain unfamiliar to most Vietnamese shoppers compared to basic lotions,
so Uvie must provide clear tutorials and visible test results before buyers trust
making patches part of their routine.

\subsection{Market Size Analysis (TAM, SAM, Target Market)}\label{sec:market-size}
To stay aligned with Section~\ref{sec:financial} (Financial Projection), this
market estimation uses the same retail price of VND 89,000 per package, unit
production cost of VND 34,000, gross profit of VND 55,000, and customer lifetime
value (LTV) model. The market sizes below count full-use demand of three packages
per user per year, which is the potential. The LTV and the sales forecast assume
about 1.6 packages per customer per year, a lifetime of two years, and an LTV of
VND 176,000 (Section~\ref{sec:financial}). Supplier sample testing will confirm the
number of patches per package, so estimates count packages sold.

Looking at the Total Addressable Market (TAM), \citet{marketsdata2023} valued
Vietnam's sun care market at USD 124.12 million in 2022 with an 8.86\% growth
rate, putting the 2025--2026 sector near USD 160 million ($\sim$VND 4,000
billion). With around 30 million Vietnamese adults aged 18 to 50 using sun
protection at an annual benchmark of $\sim$VND 133,000 per user, TAM reaches
roughly VND 4,000 billion per year.

For the Serviceable Available Market (SAM), Uvie targets young urban adults aged
18 to 35 in five major cities (Ho Chi Minh City, Hanoi, Da Nang, Can Tho, Hai
Phong) who regularly wear sunscreen and own smartphones, estimated at 4.5 million
consumers. At three packages annually (VND 267,000 per user), SAM equals
$4.5\text{ million} \times 3 \times \text{VND } 89{,}000 = \text{VND } 1{,}201.5$
billion per year ($\sim$USD 48.1 million, or 13.5 million packages).

Our three-year Target Market (SOM) matches Section~\ref{sec:financial} directly.
In Year 1, an online launch in Ho Chi Minh City targets 12,000 new customers and
18,000 packages (VND 1,602 million, 0.13\% of SAM). Year 2 expansion into Hanoi,
Da Nang, and pharmacies grows sales to 30,000 new buyers and 50,000 packages
(VND 4,450 million, 0.37\% of SAM). By Year 3, nationwide beauty chains reach
60,000 new buyers and 100,000 packages (VND 8,900 million, 0.74\% of SAM).

\subsection{Competitive Landscape Overview}\label{sec:competitors}

\subsubsection{Key Competitors Comparison}
Among direct sticker competitors, SPOTMYUV uses Dermatrue film that absorbs
sunscreen, staying clear while protected and turning purple once protection fades.
However, on its official website (Figure~\ref{fig:spotmyuv}), a 16-pack retails for
USD 16.49 ($\sim$VND 415,000, or $\sim$VND 26,000 per sticker), making daily use
unaffordable for Vietnamese students. It also lacks a phone app, leaving back or
shoulder stickers hard to check and color shifts tricky to judge in sunlight.

Past hardware wearables also reveal clear lessons on price and skin-level
accuracy. In 2018, L'Or\'eal launched the La Roche-Posay My Skin Track UV clip-on

\cite{porter2018} (Figure~\ref{fig:myskintrack}). Although innovative, it was
later discontinued because high hardware prices slowed mass adoption, and clipping
it onto clothes only tracked ambient UV rays rather than whether sunscreen on skin
had worn away.

\subsubsection{Competitive Pressure Analysis (Porter's Five Forces)}
Using Porter's Five Forces model, direct rivalry inside Vietnam's smart UV-patch
niche remains low because major cosmetics brands focus on creams and sprays rather
than verification tools. By contrast, the threat of substitutes is high since many
consumers rely on free habits like guessing a two-hour schedule, wearing
sun-protective jackets, or checking weather apps. Instead of fighting sunscreen
brands, Uvie acts as an affordable companion tool.

Across the remaining forces, buyer bargaining power is moderate because consumers
have many skincare choices, though our VND 89,000 retail price keeps trial risk
low. Supplier power is low to moderate as skin-safe hydrocolloid adhesives and
photochromic dyes are available from multiple Asian manufacturers. Finally, the
threat of new entrants is moderate: printing plain stickers is simple, yet
matching skin-safe UV gel with a reliable AI camera reader requires careful
testing.

\subsubsection{Uvie's Competitive Advantage}
Uvie builds a distinct advantage by combining an instant color-changing sticker
with a free camera-based AI web app at
\href{https://sun-check.onrender.com}{sun-check.onrender.com}. Instead of leaving
commuters or outdoor athletes to guess slight color shifts in bright glare or
inspect back patches manually, the web app evaluates patch color objectively and
triggers timely reapplication reminders inside any mobile browser without
downloads or extra gadgets.

Equally important is Uvie's student-friendly pricing in Vietnam. At VND 89,000 per
package, Uvie costs far less than imported USD 16.49 SPOTMYUV packs, USD 40--90 UV
cameras, or discontinued USD 59.95 clip-on sensors. Combining an affordable price
with simple digital verification supports repeat purchases across our urban target
segment.
